Still, it's worth comparing the headline numbers to Shopify's Finances report before you send them.

## Orders I left out (and why)

The file has 73 orders, but 6 of them shouldn't count toward August:

- **#1998–#2000 ($99.57):** these were placed on July 31, so they belong to July.
- **#2008 ($50.35):** voided and cancelled, so no money was collected.
- **#2011 and #2044 ($79.25):** paid through "Bogus Gateway", which is Shopify's test payment method. These are test orders, not real sales.

## Things to tell your bookkeeper

1. **Refunds:** #2038 was fully refunded ($46.06, which includes $6.95 shipping and $3.11 tax). #2049 was partly refunded ($12.00), but the export doesn't say what that refund was for.
2. **Gift card:** #2051 ($120.12) was paid partly with a gift card, and the export doesn't show how much. If you counted that gift card as income when it was sold, the gift card part of this order shouldn't be counted as income again. Shopify's gift card report will show the amount.
3. **Tax is charged on shipping too.** Every order is taxed at 7.25% on products plus shipping. Divide tax by product sales alone and you get about 7.7%, which is why that number looks high.
4. **Shipping:** orders of $50 or more ship free. Orders under $50 pay a flat $6.95. There were 34 free and 33 paid.
