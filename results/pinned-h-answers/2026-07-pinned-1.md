## Readings I took where your definitions leave room
These are listed as open questions in every answer until you confirm them.
1. **Payout arrival date.** I used the calendar date Stripe gives, not the date moved to New York time. This matters: Stripe's own balance summary dates payouts in New York time and gets $218,954.63, because it puts the July 1 payout of $12,200.27 into June.
2. **Unpaid.** A subscription is unpaid from the fifth failed payment attempt until it ends or its latest invoice is paid. A check confirms that none of its later invoices were attempted in that time.
3. **Paused.** A trial that ended without a card is paused until the subscription resumes on a new billing cycle.
4. **Repeating coupons.** They run from the subscription's start if they were on its first paid invoice, otherwise from the first period they discounted. The exports don't record when a coupon was applied. Because of this, some "Launch 50%" customers whose three months ran out in mid-July are at full price at the month end.
5. **Price and quantity at the month end** are rebuilt from the invoice lines, because the export shows October 3 statuses. That rebuild lands $1.4k from a rough figure taken from the October 3 export.
6. **Credit notes** are spread over the lines of the invoice they credit, in proportion to each line's amount.
7. **Fees and disputes.** A dispute fee Stripe refunds lowers that month's fees.
8. **Bank transfers.** They are Stripe's customer-balance payments on enterprise invoices, counted as cash when received.

## What happens each month
- **Rates first:** add the month's EUR and GBP rates to `month_end_rates.csv`. I entered June through September.
- **Checks:** 36 checks run on the data every time. When the tool tested the calculation on damaged copies of your files (rows doubled, last days missing, amounts in cents, shifted time zones), the answer stopped or warned in every case where a figure changed.
- **Limit:** a credit note missing from the export is caught only if it was refunded or applied before payment. A missing credit note that only went to a customer's credit balance would go unnoticed.
