**How I handled the data**
- **Left out:** 2 test orders, 2 duplicate orders, and 123 orders that were cancelled or voided.
- **Kept in:** B2B orders; phone orders; free replacement orders, as cost with no sales.
- **Matching orders to products:**
  - The Oat and Sage napkins sold under old SKU codes (`LN-NAP-OT`/`SG`). I matched them to the current `LIN-NAP-*` codes.
  - The Unique Handthrown Bowl has no SKU, so I matched it by name.
- **VAT:** I removed it using each order's own tax, since EU, UK and Swiss prices include VAT. US orders have no VAT.
- **Payment fees:**
  - Shopify Payments fees and chargebacks come from the transactions export.
  - PayPal and Klarna fees aren't in any file, so I estimated them at 3% + €0.35 per order.
- **Refunds:**
  - The data doesn't say which item in an order was refunded, so I split each refund across the order's products in proportion to their value.
  - Return rates are similar across products (2–7%), so this split doesn't change any answer.
- **Cost of refunded items:** I counted the product cost when a refunded order had shipped, and removed it when it never shipped. Assuming returned items went back into stock, or raising the PayPal/Klarna fee to 4.5%, changes margins by only about 1 point and changes no answer.
- **Product costs:** these are today's costs from the products export. If supplier costs changed since July, the figures shift.
